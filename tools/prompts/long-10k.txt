[Sequence 1 of 10, same harbor, same characters, continuity with the previous sequence]
[Shot 1, 0s-1.5s] Cinematic wide establishing shot at golden hour over a fishing harbor on a rocky northern coast. Low sun slants across the water from frame left, painting long amber reflections on gentle swells. A row of weathered wooden boats in faded blue, red and white paint knock softly against a timber pier; their ropes creak and tighten with each swell. Gulls wheel overhead and call sharply, two of them landing on a mooring post in the foreground. Thin mist hangs over the far headland, where a white lighthouse stands against a peach-colored sky streaked with high cirrus. The camera starts on a slow, steady dolly forward along the pier at waist height, shallow depth of field, anamorphic lens with gentle horizontal flares from the low sun, fine film grain, natural color with warm highlights and teal shadows.
[Shot 2, 1.5s-3s] Medium shot. An old fisherman with a short white beard, a dark green knitted wool cap, a heavy navy oilskin jacket with brass buttons and rolled sleeves, and thick rubber boots kneels on the pier beside a wooden crate of silver herring. He mends a torn orange net stretched across his knees with a wooden needle, working with practiced, unhurried movements, his weathered hands moving in rhythm. He hums a low, slow sea shanty under his breath. Behind him a younger woman in a yellow raincoat with the hood down, her dark hair tied back with a red cord, carries a coil of rope over her shoulder and walks past toward the boats, her footsteps thudding on the wet planks. Water drips steadily from the edge of the pier into the sea below. The camera holds steady at eye level with a slight handheld sway, the background softly out of focus, the lighthouse visible as a pale shape over his shoulder.
[Shot 3, 3s-4s] Close-up on the fisherman's hands: cracked knuckles, a silver ring on the left hand, salt crust in the creases of the skin, the needle pulling the twine tight with a quiet rasp. Scales from the herring glitter on the planks like coins. The sound of the shanty continues, mixed with lapping water, a distant ship horn, and the clatter of a winch somewhere off screen. Shallow focus racks from the needle to the knot as he pulls it tight.
[Shot 4, 4s-5s] Wide shot from the end of the pier looking back toward the village. The woman in the yellow raincoat steps down into a small blue rowing boat, which rocks under her weight; she sets the coil of rope on the bench and pushes off from the pier with one oar, the wooden oar thudding against the pilings. The fisherman looks up from his net, raises a hand and calls out "Mind the tide, it turns early tonight!" in a deep, gravelly voice with a northern accent. She smiles, waves back and answers "I'll be back before dark!" Her voice carries clearly over the water. The low sun is now just above the headland, the sky deepening to orange and violet, the first lights of the cottages flickering on along the shore. Gulls lift off the mooring post together with a burst of wing beats and cries.
Style and sound: grounded naturalistic cinematography, no text, no subtitles, no logos, consistent characters and wardrobe across all shots, realistic physics for water, ropes and cloth, natural skin texture. Audio: layered ambient harbor soundscape with water lapping, creaking ropes and wood, gull calls, distant ship horn and winch, footsteps on wet planks, the fisherman's humming and the two lines of dialogue clearly intelligible and lip-synced, no music score.
Lighting continuity: the key light is the low sun from frame left throughout, warm 3200 K on faces with soft falloff, cool bounce from the sea filling the shadows, a gentle rim light on the fisherman's cap and shoulders in shot 2, practical cottage lights in shot 4 adding small warm points on the far shore. Color: rich but not oversaturated, deep navy and teal water, amber and rose sky, the yellow raincoat as the single bright accent in every shot where it appears. Camera: 35 mm equivalent for wide shots, 85 mm equivalent for the medium and close shots, smooth motivated movement only, no whip pans, no zooms, no cuts other than the four shot changes described.

[Sequence 9 of 10, same harbor, same characters, continuity with the previous sequence]
[Shot 1, 0s-1.5s] Cinematic wide establishing shot at golden hour over a fishing harbor on a rocky northern coast. Low sun slants across the water from frame left, painting long amber reflections on gentle swells. A row of weathered wooden boats in faded blue, red and white paint knock softly against a timber pier; their ropes creak and tighten with each swell. Gulls wheel overhead and call sharply, two of them landing on a mooring post in the foreground. Thin mist hangs over the far headland, where a white lighthouse stands against a peach-colored sky streaked with high cirrus. The camera starts on a slow, steady dolly forward along the pier at waist height, shallow depth of field, anamorphic lens with gentle horizontal flares from the low sun, fine film grain, natural color with warm highlights and teal shadows.
[Shot 2, 1.5s-3s] Medium shot. An old fisherman with a short white beard, a dark green knitted wool cap, a heavy navy oilskin jacket with brass buttons and rolled sleeves, and thick rubber boots kneels on the pier beside a wooden crate of silver herring. He mends a torn orange net stretched across his knees with a wooden needle, working with practiced, unhurried movements, his weathered hands moving in rhythm. He hums a low, slow sea shanty under his breath. Behind him a younger woman in a yellow raincoat with the hood down, her dark hair tied back with a red cord, carries a coil of rope over her shoulder and walks past toward the boats, her footsteps thudding on the wet planks. Water drips steadily from the edge of the pier into the sea below. The camera holds steady at eye level with a slight handheld sway, the background softly out of focus, the lighthouse visible as a pale shape over his shoulder.
[Shot 3, 3s-4s] Close-up on the fisherman's hands: cracked knuckles, a silver ring on the left hand, salt crust in the creases of the skin, the needle pulling the twine tight with a quiet rasp. Scales from the herring glitter on the planks like coins. The sound of the shanty continues, mixed with lapping water, a distant ship horn, and the clatter of a winch somewhere off screen. Shallow focus racks from the needle to the knot as he pulls it tight.
[Shot 4, 4s-5s] Wide shot from the end of the pier looking back toward the village. The woman in the yellow raincoat steps down into a small blue rowing boat, which rocks under her weight; she sets the coil of rope on the bench and pushes off from the pier with one oar, the wooden oar thudding against the pilings. The fisherman looks up from his net, raises a hand and calls out "Mind the tide, it turns early tonight!" in a deep, gravelly voice with a northern accent. She smiles, waves back and answers "I'll be back before dark!" Her voice carries clearly over the water. The low sun is now just above the headland, the sky deepening to orange and violet, the first lights of the cottages flickering on along the shore. Gulls lift off the mooring post together with a burst of wing beats and cries.
